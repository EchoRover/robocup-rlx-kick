RL-X kick and get-up: what I tried and why each step failed

Evan Tobias · ACOD310 RoboCup 3D (Booster T1) · 9 Oct 2026 · full details, tables and code

Kick

24 Sep · try 1
Tried
Built a kick environment in RL-X: copied its RoboCup walking environment, added a ball and a kick reward (ball speed toward the goal, foot near the ball, stay up). Trained on the HPC CPUs.
Result
~500 steps per second. Useless.
Why
JAX/MJX barely uses a CPU (about 1.4 of 64 cores). It is built for GPUs.
Next
Moved to an A100 GPU: ~75,000 steps per second, 4,096 robots at once.
24-25 Sep · kick v1 (GPU)
Result
"Success" climbed, then training crashed (NaN).
Why
Reward hacking. It learned to stomp the ball into the floor until the physics exploded. The exploding ball had a huge "speed", so the reward paid for it.
Fix
End the episode when the physics blows up, cap the reward, and count success only for a real 1-15 m kick.
25 Sep · kick v2 (GPU, ~1.7 billion steps)
Result
A real kick: weight shift, swing, ball flies. 64/64 kicks, 0 falls, ~28 m (calm starts).
But
From messy starts (tilted, joints off, already moving) it falls 45 of 64.
8 Oct · why was it fragile?
Found
The curriculum (which makes starts messier as the robot succeeds) was stuck at 0.10.
Why
My success rule said 1-15 m. The kick goes ~28 m, so every good kick counted as a failure, and difficulty never went up.
Fix (v3)
Success = ball at least 1 m forward and the robot doesn't fall, no cap. The curriculum then climbed 0 → 0.85 in 20 minutes.
9 Oct · into the real competition server
Tried
Exported the policy, wrote a client that builds the same inputs from the server's messages, and ran 12 fixed kick setups in the unmodified server.
Result
Falls 12 out of 12. Ball only ~3.5 m.
Why
The training simulator resets every joint's speed to its motor limit after each step. The real server doesn't. The kick learned to swing harder than real physics allows, and in the server the swing throws the robot over.
Proof
Added only that reset to the server: falls went from 12 to 1. Second biggest difference: the ball (weight, bounce, rolling).
9 Oct night · kick v4 (laptop CPU, 25 min)
Tried
Retrained from v2 without the speed reset and with the server's ball.
Result
Server, no delay: 0/12 falls, ~20 m. With the 20 ms delay a real match has: 12/12 falls.
Why
It had never practised with delay. In RL-X the delay only grows with the curriculum, and v4's curriculum was still low.
9 Oct night · kick v5 (laptop CPU, ~2.5 h)
Tried
Same as v4, with the 20 ms delay always on.
Result
Server with match timing: 11/12 kicks, 1/12 falls, ~20 m, 3° off target.
Lesson
Training longer is not always better: v4 got worse with more training, so I tested every saved checkpoint in the server.
still failing · the camera
Result
Using the robot's camera instead of the exact ball position: it kicks, then falls (10/12).
Why
The server's camera has a 60° field of view and noise. The ball leaves the view right after contact, so the policy sees an old ball position. It was trained with a perfect ball position 50 times a second.
Next
Train with a camera-like ball (noise, gaps, loses the ball after contact): the teacher-student method from UT Austin's T1 striker paper.

Get-up

24 Sep · building it
Tried
Get-up environment: start tilted or lying (up to 90°, growing with the curriculum), 10 s to stand. Reward: torso height, being upright, a bonus once standing, then holding still.
Bug
The fallen robot sank through the floor.
Why
The walking environment removes every collision shape except the feet (to run faster). A walking robot only touches the floor with its feet; a fallen one doesn't.
Fix
Kept the body's collision shapes in the get-up environment.
25 Sep · get-up v1 (GPU)
Result
Learned to recover from small tilts only. The curriculum stuck at ~0.17 (about 15° starts).
Why
Moving up needs a streak of successes, and success was only ~55%, so the streaks never came.
9 Oct · tested over the full 10 s
Result
Near-upright starts: 64/64 standing. From lying: 0/64. It gets to sitting or kneeling and stops.
Why
Kneeling already earns most of the height and upright reward. Getting from kneeling to standing needs a big, forceful push, and the penalties on force and joint speed discourage exactly that. So it settles for "good enough" (a local optimum). Some lying starts also crashed the physics; I added a guard.
Now
Two GPU runs since 9:12 today. A: always start lying, so it practises the hard case. B: same, with the force/speed penalties cut 10×, the first stage of the HumanUP paper (first find any way up, then make it smooth).

What I'd say I learned

1. A policy that is perfect in training can fail completely in the real server because of a hidden simulator shortcut. Test in the real server early.

2. Change one difference at a time to find the real cause.

3. Check your success rule and curriculum: mine silently stopped training from getting harder.

4. Reward hacking is real: the robot found a physics bug before it found a kick.
