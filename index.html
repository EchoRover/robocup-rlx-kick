Teaching a robot to kick and get up

Evan Tobias · RoboCup 3D, Booster T1 · trained with reinforcement learning (PPO) in RL-X · details and code

1 · Before: my kick falls over in the real match server

  • In practice (the training simulator) this kick was near perfect: ball flies about 28 m, robot stays up.
  • In the real match server it falls 12 out of 12 times.
  • Why: the practice simulator secretly slows every joint down to its speed limit after each step, like a safety rope. The real server has no rope. So the robot learned to swing as hard as it can, and in the real server that swing throws it over.
  • How I know: I added only that "rope" to the server, nothing else, and falls went from 12 to 1.

2 · After: it kicks in the real server

  • What I changed: retrained with the rope removed, with the server's real ball, and with the 20 ms delay a real match has.
  • Result in the real server: 11 out of 12 kicks, only 1 fall, about 20 m, almost straight at the target.
  • Still to fix: with the robot's own camera it kicks and then falls, because the ball disappears from view right after the kick. Next: practise with a camera that's just as bad.

3 · Why it doesn't get up

  • It starts lying down, gets to kneeling, and stays down. From lying: 0 out of 64. From a small wobble: 64 out of 64.
  • Reason 1, it barely practised lying down: practice only gets harder after a streak of successes. It kept failing, so it mostly practised small wobbles.
  • Reason 2, kneeling is "good enough": kneeling already earns most of the points (head fairly high, body fairly upright). Standing up needs one big, forceful push, and I give minus points for forceful moves. So it settles for kneeling.
  • Fix running now on the GPU: always start lying down, and much smaller minus points for force, so it can push hard first and get smooth later.
